What is TenshiNet?

TenshiNet (stylized tenshi_NET) is the name of my home lab.

It’s based off of an alias which, in turn, is based off of an older alias (Seraph) that I used to use in games like flight simulators as my callsign. When I started learning hacking, I decided to update that alias. I wanted to keep the meaning roughly the same, but with an update to reflect my interests today. I’ve developed an interest in Japanese culture in recent years, and because Japanese culture tends to be involved in tech thrillers (including cyberpunk fiction), I decided to try to convert Seraph into Japanese. Unfortunately, I couldn’t figure out a direct translation of “Seraph” to Japanese, so Tenshi was the closest I could come.

Beyond that, I decided to use my home lab’s name for my GitHub, my blog and my TryHackMe account in an attempt to essentially treat them as an extension of my home lab. I don’t view them as separate, functionally speaking, even if they’re not running on my own hardware.

About me

TryHackMe | GitHub

I’m Jacob (alias: Tenshi), a lifelong writer and tech hobbyist turned professional. Following several attempted careers, in 2023, I embarked on a journey of education in IT with the goal of entering the information security industry, indulging upon long-term curiosity toward computers and digital technology.

My goal is to leverage my talents and my passion for technology to build a lifetime career. I am obsessed with tinkering and learning new things; I’m the sort who will geek out over the cultivation of tea, the history of the blue LED, slight differences between Linux distros, even the quirks of long-dead DOS viruses.

I started this blog to discuss and document my progress in learning IT/security topics, to showcase projects and home lab planning, to discuss opinions (on various subjects) and so forth. I might also chat about tea from time to time, as Japanese and Chinese tea cultures are a hobby that’s important to me!

Things to expect

The main focus is Information Technology and cybersecurity, with a focus on ethical hacking, SBCs and microcontrollers. This also extends into Linux and open source software, from opinions to troubleshooting to philosophy.

The secondary focus would be home lab projects, including Raspberry Pi builds, DIY electronics solutions and careful smart home/IoT builds. Don’t expect pro-level electrical engineering; no wild robotics or anything like that, just practical and cautiously-crafted builds.

On the subject of cautious implementation of IoT, I’ll also touch on digital privacy and overall tech opinions, including occasional discussions on controversial topics like AI, Web3 and so forth. However, one thing you don’t have to worry about is clickbait: I always try to maintain an objective approach rather than a sensationalist one when approaching topics like those.
